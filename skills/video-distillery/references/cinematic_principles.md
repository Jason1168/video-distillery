## 1. SHOT LANGUAGE

### Shot Sizes (Closest to Farthest)
| Code | Name | Framing | Emotional Effect |
|------|------|---------|-----------------|
| ECU | Extreme Close-Up | Single eye, lips, hand detail | Maximum intimacy, tension |
| CU | Close-Up | Full face / hands | Personal, emotional revelation |
| MCU | Medium Close-Up | Chest up | Conversational intimacy |
| MS | Medium Shot | Waist up | Neutral, dialogue default |
| MWS | Medium Wide Shot | Full figure + environment | Context with character |
| WS | Wide Shot | Full figure, environment prominent | Location, isolation, scale |
| VWS | Very Wide Shot | Tiny figure in landscape | Overwhelm, freedom, loneliness |
| EWS | Extreme Wide Shot / Establishing | No clear figure | Pure environment, world-building |

**Replication Rule**: Count the ratio of CU:MS:WS in the reference. Match within ±15%.

### Camera Movements
| Movement | Feel | When to Use |
|----------|------|-------------|
| **Static/Locked** | Stability, dread, observation | Quiet moments, confrontations |
| **Pan** | Following, surveying | Character reveal, landscape |
| **Tilt** | Power reveal, scale | Architecture, intimidation |
| **Dolly In** | Focus, intimacy, gravity | Emotional peak moments |
| **Dolly Out** | Revelation, isolation, loss | Story-ending pull-back |
| **Tracking/Follow** | Energy, momentum, presence | Action, pursuit, urgency |
| **Handheld** | Documentary feel, anxiety, authenticity | Drama, intimacy, chaos |
| **Drone/Aerial** | God's eye, context, grandeur | Transition, establishing, spectacle |
| **Slider** | Elegant movement, production value | Product, beauty, slow reveals |
| **Crane/Jib** | Drama, theatrical, spectacle | Climax, celebration, endings |

### Lens Focal Lengths & Their Feel
| Range | Feel | Use |
|-------|------|-----|
| 14–24mm | Wide, distorted edges, immersive | Environment, action, GoPro |
| 24–35mm | Near-natural, slightly wide | Vérité documentary, intimate WS |
| 50mm | Natural human eye, neutral | Dialogue, everyday moments |
| 85mm | Flattering compression, shallow DOF | Portraits, hero moments |
| 135mm+ | Strong compression, background blur | Telephoto drama, wildlife |

## 2. EDITING PRINCIPLES

### Pacing & Rhythm
- **Average Shot Length (ASL)**: Most commercial/brand films: 3–5s. Art films: 10–30s. Action: <2s.
- **Music Sync**: Cuts on beat = energetic. Cuts slightly off-beat = tension.
- **Pacing Curve**: Build from slow → fast → resolve slow. Or slow throughout for meditative pieces.

### Cut Types
| Cut | Effect | Use Case |
|-----|--------|----------|
| **Hard Cut** | Clean, modern, neutral | Default for most edits |
| **Match Cut** | Visual continuity, elegance | Shape/motion matches across scenes |
| **Jump Cut** | Energy, urgency, time compression | Action, music videos |
| **J-Cut** | Audio leads visuals | Dialogue scenes, smooth scene transitions |
| **L-Cut** | Visuals lead audio | Lingering on a reaction |
| **Cross-Cut** | Parallel action, tension | Chase scenes, parallel storylines |
| **Dissolve** | Time passing, memory, dream | Flashbacks, montages |
| **Smash Cut** | Shock, humor, contrast | Comedy, horror reveals |

### The 6 Rules of Editing (Walter Murch)
1. **Emotion** (51%) — Does it serve the emotional moment?
2. **Story** (23%) — Does it advance the story?
3. **Rhythm** (10%) — Does it happen at the right moment?
4. **Eye Trace** (7%) — Where are eyes looking?
5. **2D Plane** (5%) — Does it respect screen direction?
6. **3D Space** (4%) — Does it make spatial sense?

## 3. COLOR GRADING

### Color Temperatures
- **Warm (2700–4000K)**: Golden hour, nostalgia, comfort, intimacy
- **Neutral (5000–5600K)**: Natural, documentary, authentic
- **Cool (6500–8000K)**: Corporate, cold, futuristic, clinical

### Common Grade Styles
| Style | Description | Reference Films |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| **Teal & Orange** | Skin tones orange, shadows teal | Hollywood blockbusters |
| **Muted/Desaturated** | Low saturation, lifted blacks | Drama, indie film |
| **High Contrast** | Deep blacks, bright whites | Thriller, noir |
| **Natural/Flat** | Minimal grade, true-to-life | Documentary, vérité |
| **Pastel** | Soft, low contrast, airy | Lifestyle, fashion |
| **Moody Dark** | Crushed blacks, desaturated | Horror, dark drama |
| **Warm Vintage** | Faded highlights, warm shadows | Period pieces, nostalgia |
| **High Fashion** | Bold, graphic, high saturation | Luxury brands |

### Color Psychology
- **Red**: Urgency, danger, passion, power
- **Blue**: Trust, calm, sadness, cold
- **Green**: Nature, growth, envy, mystery
- **Yellow/Gold**: Optimism, warmth, luxury
- **Purple**: Mystery, royalty, spirituality
- **Black**: Elegance, death, power
- **White**: Purity, clinical, minimalism

## 4. SOUND DESIGN HIERARCHY

```
Master Mix
├── Music (carrier of emotion)
│   ├── Score (custom-composed)
│   └── Licensed (pre-existing)
├── Dialogue / Narration (information)
├── Sound Effects
│   ├── Hard SFX (footsteps, doors, specific actions)
│   ├── Ambient / Room Tone (environment foundation)
│   └── Design SFX (stylized, non-realistic)
└── Silence (the most powerful tool)
```

### Music Selection Principles
- **Tempo match**: Music BPM should suggest the visual rhythm target
- **Emotional lead**: Music should arrive 1–2 seconds before the emotion peaks
- **Less is more**: Silence before music creates maximum impact
- **Genre fit**: Match music to world of the story, not just the mood

## 5. LIGHTING SETUPS

### Key Lighting Styles
| Style | Feel | Technical Setup |
|-------|------|----------------|
| **Golden Hour** | Warm, magical, cinematic | 1 hour after sunrise/before sunset |
| **Soft Box / Diffused** | Commercial, flattering, clean | Large diffused key light |
| **Rembrandt** | Dramatic, classical, moody | 45° key, shadow triangle on cheek |
| **Backlit / Rim** | Silhouette, mystery, style | Strong back light, little fill |
| **Practical Lighting** | Authentic, natural, immersive | Use actual lamps, windows |
| **High Key** | Commercial, bright, optimistic | Multiple lights, minimal shadow |
| **Low Key** | Dramatic, noir, moody | Single key, deep shadows |
| **Natural Light** | Documentary, authentic | Window light, open shade |

## 6. THE 180° RULE & SPATIAL CONTINUITY
Always maintain consistent screen direction within a scene.
Breaking it intentionally = disorientation (use with purpose).

## 7. RULE OF THIRDS
Primary subject at intersection points of a 3×3 grid.
Breaking it (centering) = Kubrickian power, formalism, unease.

## 8. ASPECT RATIOS & THEIR MEANING
- **16:9** (1.78:1) — Standard, digital native, YouTube
- **2.39:1** (Anamorphic/Scope) — Cinematic, epic, grandeur
- **1.85:1** (Flat) — Classic Hollywood
- **1:1** (Square) — Social, intimate, Instagram
- **4:3** — Vintage, home video nostalgia, intimate
- **2.76:1** (Ultra Panavision) — Ultra-epic (Ben-Hur, Hateful Eight)
